# Frequency Baseline — how often each theme actually appears

**This file is evidence, not interpretation.** It answers one question: when sleep apnea sufferers post without being prompted by my search terms, what do they actually talk about, and how often?

## How to read this

- Source: **Track A only** — every post published on 34 calendar days spread evenly across ~18 months (Mar 2025 → Aug 2026) in r/SleepApnea and r/CPAP, **no keyword filter**. 68 day-units, all complete.
- Posts counted: **1,976** posts with more than 150 characters of body text (2,422 posts total in the sample; short/link-only posts excluded).
- A post can carry several themes, so columns do not sum to 100%.
- Detection is automatic pattern-matching over title + body, spot-checked by reading. It detects **topic mentioned**, not **how badly it hurts**. A 4.9% theme can still be the most emotionally violent thing in the market — see the intensity note at the bottom.

### The single most useful finding in this table

**The two subreddits are two different markets, not one.**

- **r/SleepApnea = the deciding market.** Diagnosis journey 29%, fatigue 24%, snoring 15%, surgery 12%, oral appliances 8.8% — people working out *what is wrong with me and what do I do about it*.
- **r/CPAP = the operating market.** Leaks 14.5%, cleaning 12.7%, mask comfort 14.6% — people who already own the device and are fighting the machine, not the diagnosis.

Anything sold to "sleep apnea sufferers" as one audience is being sold to two audiences with almost no shared vocabulary. Confidence: **High** (large unbiased sample, clean separation, consistent across the whole 18-month window).

## 3. Intensity vs frequency — the trap in this table

The bottom four rows are the ones to be careful with, in **both** directions:

**Don't inflate them.** "I thought I was lazy for ten years", "I was scared I'd die in my sleep", "no doctor believed me" are the most quotable lines in the entire corpus and they are *everywhere* in the targeted search. In the unbiased sample they appear in 0.1–0.5% of posts. Anyone who reads only the targeted quotes will build a market model out of the loudest 1% and then wonder why the ads don't convert. **The emotional extremes are real, vivid, and rare.**

**Don't dismiss them either.** People rarely narrate their self-image in a post about mask sizing — the low count reflects what a Reddit post is *for*, not how common the feeling is. These lines cluster where people are telling their whole story (diagnosis posts, milestone posts, "I'm at my breaking point" posts).

Sound reading: use frequent themes to decide **what to talk about**, and rare-but-intense language to decide **how to say it** — never the reverse.

## 4. What this baseline cannot tell you

1. **Survivorship bias.** People who quit CPAP and moved on largely stop posting. The true quit rate in the market is far higher than the 1.8% of posts that mention quitting; clinical literature has long put CPAP non-adherence in the tens of percent. This corpus systematically under-counts the people who gave up.
2. **No Facebook layer** (see methodology §1). Facebook groups skew older, more partner-involved, more device-focused.
3. **English-language, online, help-seeking population only.**
4. **Topic ≠ pain.** Cleaning appears more often than the fear of dying. That ranks *posting frequency*, not *market pain*.
5. The **25% "data tinkering"** number is partly a Reddit/CPAPtalk community artifact: these forums select for people who download OSCAR and read their own charts. It is a real and reachable segment, but do not read it as "a quarter of all apnea patients analyse their data".
