# RAW EVIDENCE — What sufferers tell each other

Posts show what people are willing to say about themselves. **Comments show what the market believes**, because that is where sufferers answer each other, correct each other, and hand out the advice that has already shaped every objection you will meet in an ad.

**Source:** **11,528 comments from 163 of the highest-discussion threads** in the corpus, retrieved thread by thread, plus 300 comments from keyword searches. See `raw/reddit-comments.jsonl` (the `_src` field distinguishes `thread:<postid>` from `kw:<keyword>`).

---

## 1. The community's standing advice — what they say to almost every problem

Measured across all 11,528 thread comments (share containing each pattern). The percentages are small because most comments are conversation, not advice — the *ranking* is the useful part, and it is stable: the same order held on a quarter of the data.

| Advice pattern | Share of comments | n |
|---|---:|---:|
| Try a different mask / cushion / size | 4.7% | 537 |
| Change your settings (EPR, ramp, humidity, heated tube, pressure) | 4.4% | 510 |
| Consider an alternative (BiPAP, ASV, oral appliance, Inspire, surgery) | 4.1% | 472 |
| Fix mouth leak (tape, chin strap, collar) | 2.5% | 283 |
| Get your data (OSCAR, SleepHQ, MyAir) | 1.9% | 216 |
| Give it time / don't give up | 1.7% | 194 |
| See an ENT / check the nose | 1.4% | 159 |
| Consider weight / GLP-1 | 1.3% | 151 |
| Look for another cause (iron, thyroid, B12, narcolepsy) | 0.8% | 90 |
| Solidarity, no advice | 0.8% | 89 |

**The commercially important consequence:** by the time anyone sees your ad, they have already been told *"try a different mask"* and *"adjust your settings"* — repeatedly, by peers, for free. That advice is the market's default. Any product whose pitch resembles it lands as noise. This is the mechanism behind the strongest objection in the research: **"I've already tried everything like that."**

Confidence: **High** on the ranking; the percentages are a floor, since advice is often phrased without keywords.

## 2. "What support did you wish you had?" — answered by 211 people

The thread: https://www.reddit.com/r/CPAP/comments/1ohzhlr/ — *"It seems crazy that people are handed a complex medical device with little to no follow-up support."*

The answers are unusually concrete. This is a market telling you its product requirements:

> "**A one page visual of what to clean and on what schedule.** (E.g. daily: clean these, weekly: clean this). A one page easy to read visual of **what supplies to order and replace on what schedule and how to order them**... These pieces are what has been most overwhelming for me to figure out"

> "US here, got my CPAP via Kaiser. I think I've had it for about **5 weeks now, and I haven't heard a word from Kaiser for follow-up** as of today. **No mention of replacement supplies, no mention of adjustment of pressure or interpretation/feedback on readings.** I also had to request the heated tube after I realized they didn't prescribe [it]"

> "**Honestly just making the effort to let me try all the masks!!**"

> "**I wish I was told to keep trying masks. That the first one may not be the right one.** ... **I kept blaming myself wondering what I was doing wrong** until I found the N20 air touch. **I was going insane with exhaustion.**"

> "**I wish I had literally any support or instruction.** My sleep doctor sent me to a place where he said I'd be 100% covered by insurance and they would teach me how to use it and let me try different masks. Later, that day I got a call that the insurance wasn't even covering my visit with him. And that they '**never cover machines**.'"

> "Same I had my follow up since compliance, and **all he basically said we[re] everything looks good and sent me on my way**"

**Extraction:**
- **Unmet needs, stated in the customer's own words:** a cleaning schedule on one page; a supply-replacement schedule with ordering instructions; permission and opportunity to try multiple masks; someone to interpret their data; a follow-up that isn't a rubber stamp.
- **Emotional core:** *"I kept blaming myself wondering what I was doing wrong."* The support vacuum converts a solvable equipment problem into personal failure — and personal failure is what makes people quit.
- **Confidence: High.** These are direct answers to a direct question, from many independent people, in one place.

## 3. How the community answers the fear of being undateable

Responding to *"31, single... am I really gonna find someone with a CPAP?"* (https://www.reddit.com/r/CPAP/comments/1l7icdx/, 203 comments):

> "Bro same. **The alcoholism is way more a problem than my Vader mask lol.** Plus **good women don't give af about something that keeps you alive** and they don't have to hear me snore lol"

- **Note the mechanism:** the community reframes the machine from *defect* to *proof of self-care*, and points out the alternative is snoring next to them. That reframe is peer-generated and already circulating — which makes it credible ground for creative, and also means it is not a novel insight to this audience.
- Confidence: **Medium** — one strongly upvoted reply in a large thread; the reframe is consistent with the broader "the club" culture documented in file 06.

## 4. The community's own critique of sleep medicine

> "**The biggest problem with sleep medicine at present is how it assumes that everyone is the same and this sets people up to fail.** When done well, PAP therapy is damned near miraculous (I wake up and fall asleep easily for literally the first time in my life, my mental health is improved greatly, my hands and feet are warm[)]"

> "Apnea is **1 of literally 50 things that can cause fatigue.**"

> "That's wild, I was diagnosed with mild apnea and after continuing to have fatigue and more tests, **just recently diagnosed with anemia**... currently being treated for the anemia and **it's helping much more than the CPAP did**"

> "Yep, **you need to be annoying.** I couldn't figure out why I was still getting so many events per hour with no mask leaks... I discovered that **90+ percent weren't obstructive apnea they were central.** All my efforts weren't doing anything."

**Extraction:**
- **Belief:** one-size-fits-all treatment is the failure mode; individual variation is the whole game
- **Belief:** apnea is one candidate cause of fatigue among many — this community runs its own differential diagnosis
- **Belief:** you must self-advocate aggressively ("you need to be annoying") to get correct care
- **Consequence for advertising:** claims that assume a uniform patient will be met with "that's exactly what's wrong with this field". Specificity about *who this is for and who it isn't* is the trust signal here.
- Confidence: **Medium-High.**

## 5. Practical fixes people share that reveal hidden pains

> "I turned off the heated tube and turned my humidity down to like 1-2 from the default 6. Once I stopped **breathing swamp gas humid air** I slept so, so much better."

> "**I've Pavlov'd myself with mine. Mask on? Brain says, okay you're very sleepy now.**"

> "I have a CPAP pillow it's fantastic. I know exactly what the person was saying about the **nightly negotiation with the pillow** lol. **Cut out pillow saved it all.**"

> "it does worsen reflux actually. would be a nightmare to need this for someone suffering terribly of their reflux already"

> "I get anxious and claustrophobic but **once I'm able to pass that stage** of either going to sleep or waking up in the middle of the night, I'm good. **My events went from 25 to 1-4 a night.** I feel more rested, energetic, and in a better mood"

**Extraction:** default settings are frequently wrong for the individual; conditioning the brain to associate the mask with sleep is a real user strategy; pillow geometry is a genuine fix people rave about; reflux is an under-discussed comorbidity; and claustrophobia is often a *transitional* barrier rather than a permanent one — people describe passing through it.

## 6. What comments add that posts do not

1. **Posts are written in crisis; comments are written in hindsight.** The most useful causal information — what actually fixed it — lives in comments.
2. **The objection-handling is already done.** Peers pre-empt fear-mongering, weight talk and miracle claims before an advertiser arrives.
3. **The advice ceiling is visible.** The community can offer masks, settings, and solidarity. It cannot offer clinical access, money, or an explanation when the numbers look fine. **Those three gaps are where an outside product has something to add** — and, not coincidentally, they map onto the strongest angles in `ANGLES/`.
