# Ninety Nights

**Voice-of-customer research — Global Sleep Apnea Support Community (GSASC), Facebook**

What 96,000 people say to each other about sleep apnea when no clinician is listening: the pains, the failed fixes, the objections, and the exact words they use.

| | |
|---|---|
| Group members | 96,236 |
| Posts read | ~150 |
| Full comment threads opened | 10 |
| Feed window | ~7 days |
| Collected | 21 August 2026 |

---

## 1. Six things this community makes obvious

Read in bulk, the feed is not a device forum. It is a support group for an adaptation crisis that the healthcare system hands over and then walks away from.

1. **Nobody owns the first 30 nights.** The supplier drops off a box, the doctor disappears for 6–8 weeks, and a Facebook group becomes the de facto onboarding programme.
2. **Adaptation is measured in months.** The community's honest answer to "how long?" is three months to a year. New users arrive expecting three nights.
3. **Mask fit is trial-and-error paid in skin.** Four to ten masks is normal. People report nose sores, bruised bridges, chapped lips, black eyes and pinched nerves before they land.
4. **Compliant, low AHI, still exhausted.** A large, quiet group does everything right, scores 0–2 events, and feels no better. Nobody offers them a next step.
5. **They have numbers, not meaning.** Pressure, AHI, leak rate, sleep-stage graphs. Members can read every figure and interpret none of them, so they ask strangers.
6. **Fear is the community's persuasion tool.** Doubt is answered with death, stroke and dementia. It keeps people compliant and simultaneously makes quitters go quiet.

## 2. Where the journey breaks

Posts sort cleanly into phases. Each has its own vocabulary, its own panic, and its own drop-out risk.

### Pre-diagnosis — years of unexplained exhaustion
Fatigue gets attributed to weight, thyroid, ADHD, laziness or age. Diagnosis arrives with relief and grief in the same breath.

> "my muscles are made of lead and it's impossible to get up, it feels like being a human sandbag"
> — anonymous member, chronically exhausted since age 10

### Handover — a box, a briefing, a range of 4–20
Equipment arrives from a DME supplier with minimal explanation. Settings are a mystery from night one; some machines even arrive showing a previous patient's data.

> "Today, my pressures were increased to 16. I have no idea what this means, and I also don't understand what pressures mean or what they do."
> — member switched to BiPAP for central apnea

### Nights 1–14 — the panic window
Sleeplessness gets worse before it gets better. This is where returns happen, and where the group's traffic concentrates.

> "Haven't been able to sleep more than 3 hours in 3 days… My body just will not relax."
> — new user, day 14

> "I want to return the machine after 2 nights. What's the cost"
> — post that drew 269 replies

### Weeks 2–12 — mask roulette, on a clock
Cycling masks while an insurance trial window ticks. Fit packs are frequently not covered; the wrong mask costs skin and sleep.

> "I have tried ten different masks and that seems to be the best one."
> — member with a macular hole, told eye leaks are "an absolute NONO"

> "my understanding is insurance won't pay for an upgraded mask before the 90 day trial is completed?"
> — member, days into therapy, cannot side-sleep in an F20

### Month 3+ — the fork
Either the numbers and the mask click and the member becomes an evangelist — or the numbers are perfect and the tiredness isn't gone, and faith collapses.

> "Good night. 0.0 events. F40 mask."
> — a member's entire post, that day

> "Almost 3 months in CPAP therapy… AHI is always below 2. sleeping 8-9 hrs daily straight on CPAP but still feeling tired, low energy, fatigued."
> — member, all bloodwork normal

### Year 1+ — life sentence or lifeline
Long-timers split into two stable camps that both post constantly: those who cannot sleep without it, and those who still hate it every night and use it anyway.

> "3 years and I still hate it. But I use it for the health benefits."

## 3. Pains — what actually hurts

Ranked by how often the theme recurred. *Constant* = most days of the feed. *Recurring* = several independent posts. *Notable* = fewer posts, unusually intense language.

### Constant

**Mask leak & fit** — the single most-posted topic. Leaks at the lower lip, the nose bridge, the cheek when side-sleeping.
> "it just leaked air at the bottom of the mask ~ I tightened the straps and repositioned the mask multiple times and it just kept blowing out"

**Dry mouth & mouth leak** — escalates to dental damage. Drives a whole economy of tape, chin straps, lozenges and balms.
> "My mouth is so dry my lips stick to my teeth" · "My dentist says it is leading to tooth decay"

**Facial damage** — nose sores, bruised bridge, pimples, chapped lips, strap marks, morning puffiness.
> "I would get up some mornings and look like I had been punched in the eye"

**Can't sleep in the position they sleep in** — stomach and side sleepers are effectively asked to change how they sleep.
> "I always slept on my stomach before and I feel like the cpap is making it impossible for me to sleep now"

**Treated but still tired** — the most emotionally loaded pain in the group, because it invalidates the whole bargain.
> "Been using it for almost 10 years. Still waiting for that miracle."

### Recurring

**Nobody explains the numbers** — members post raw sleep-study printouts and machine data and ask the group to interpret them.
> "What should I think of these results?" · "Please help me understand."

**Supplier & clinician neglect** — unanswered messages, wrong orders, unwanted auto-shipments, no follow-up.
> "having to pretty much stalk them down everytime I need something"

**Cost and coverage anxiety** — rental terms, 90-day mask windows, uncovered fit packs, losing insurance mid-therapy.
> "I can't afford to swap machines so pleas don't suggest that"

**Aerophagia & pressure intolerance** — air swallowing, bloating, cramping, cheeks puffing, pressure blowing the mask off the face.
> "my belly fills the snoopy Balloon in the Macy's parade"

**Weight, blame and shame** — "lose weight" delivered as the whole treatment plan; plus a fear that CPAP itself causes weight gain.
> "All those years I felt guilty because I couldn't lose weight."

**Insomnia stacked on apnea** — two conditions treated as one. The mask becomes a stimulus in an already broken sleep-onset routine.
> "The Insomnia is just something I have to live through, just like the Sleep Apnea."

**Noise, hose, rainout, logistics** — water in the mask, hose drag, machine placement, cleaning regimes, travel, power outages.
> "the hose near my face is noisy as or feels that way and keeps me awake"

### Notable

**Panic, claustrophobia, trauma** — a bad early episode conditions fear of the mask itself. Also PTSD, medical trauma, sensory sensitivity.
> "I now associate the mask with that experience… I'm therefore struggling to even use it, despite really wanting to make CPAP work."

**Identity, intimacy, stigma** — rarely posted, heavily engaged when it is.
> "How the hell does one go into the dating scene after being in this stigma situation?"

**Driving licence & livelihood** — real fear, wildly inconsistent information by country and state. Produces avoidance behaviour.
> "Don't ever tell a doctor that you are sleepy during the day."

## 4. Desires — what they actually want

Not "better therapy". The wishes are specific, modest and emotional — and almost none are about the machine.

> "If you could wake up tomorrow and have ONE thing about your sleep apnea completely fixed, what would it be, and why?"
> — the question a new member put to the group

> "If I could fix one thing, it would be waking up every morning knowing with certainty that I had truly restorative sleep."
> — the top answer

> "I'm so excited to see what a good nights sleep is like."
> — member collecting a first machine after years of an hour's sleep a night

> "actually have dreams!!" — member, eight months in

> "I got enough energy back into my life that I begin walking every day" — member, down 118 lb

> "he doesn't worry about whether I'm going to start breathing again!" — member describing her husband

Four repeated functional asks underneath:

- **Certainty that it is working** — not a compliance score, an answer to "am I actually better?". The daily number is a proxy people cling to because nothing else is offered.
- **Control over their own settings and data** — recurring requests for machines "that allow the user to control the settings", and for AirView / SD-card reports their clinic won't hand over.
- **A trustworthy way to buy** — "we are willing to pay out of pocket. we want a supplier we can trust."
- **To sleep the way they used to sleep** — on their stomach, on their side, next to their partner, without a strap across their face.

## 5. Failed fixes — what they've already tried and abandoned

Any new offer lands on top of this graveyard.

### Clinical alternatives
- **Mandibular / dental devices** — "I tried the dental device. It ruined the bite in my mouth." Others say it works but is uncomfortable even when properly fitted.
- **Inspire implant** — high interest, high fear. In-thread objections: MRI incompatibility, nerve damage rumours, and that it isn't for central apnea.
- **Surgery** — "the horror stories I've read in this group"; a deviated-septum repair described as risky and likely to "eventually grow back".
- **The apnea pill** — asked about repeatedly, answered with "It's not FDA approved yet" and "If there was a pill I'm pretty sure we would all be taking it."
- **Weight loss** — the most-prescribed and most-resented. GLP-1s are the current hope; one member reports zero obstructive apneas at goal weight, another lost 75 lb with no change at all.

### Self-treatment instead of therapy
- **Mouth tape alone** — a live and contested question. The community shuts it down hard, sometimes with gallows humour: "Yes it works very well. Have your affairs in order before bed."
- **Sleep trackers as diagnostics** — members conclude they're cured because a phone app stopped logging snoring. The reply: "No snoring doesn't mean no sleep apnea."
- **Sleep aids** — "I've tried Zopiclone, Melatonin, Sleep Tea, Cherry Active, Nytol, everything." Melatonin megadoses (up to 30 mg) are common and quietly harmful.

### The home-brew layer
An enormous amount of unofficial engineering happens between the mask and the face — the clearest signal that the supplied product is incomplete:

- Gauze and bandages taped over the nose bridge; nipple cream, Cortizone ointment and Paw Paw ointment inside the nostrils; a wad of tissue jammed under the cushion to change the seal angle.
- An eye mask tucked into the gap so leaked air doesn't dry the eyes; safety glasses worn indoors under a ceiling fan.
- Satin sewn into a chin strap because the fabric is unbearable; a home-made buckwheat-hull pillow shaped so the lower pillow misses the shoulder.
- Hair tied into a ponytail and threaded under the headgear to stop it riding up; hose slung over a coat hanger, a door, or "lined up between the boobs" to kill noise.
- Buying a refurbished machine outright specifically to escape insurance compliance monitoring.

## 6. Objections — the exact sentences that precede quitting

Each appeared verbatim, usually as the first line of a post.

> "I absolutely hate it so far I can't sleep" — day 3

> "I'm not a fan of CPAP, has anyone tried the other stuff?" — opening line of a 71-comment thread

> "I figured after 3 days I'd have no events." — expectation mismatch, stated plainly

> "I decided to do without for 3 days. My sleep score went up into the high 70-80s… I just don't see the benefits of using it. Maybe it's not for me." — 13 months of near-perfect compliance

> "I'm able to sleep well without the cpap." — member questioning a low starting pressure

> "I get one exchange and I am afraid the next mask might be worse." — 73-year-old, first mask

> "I don't like wearing it. If I didn't snore I would give it up completely. There has to be a better way"

> "Is there a place I can just buy machines and supplies without all the nonsense?" — member describing a year of unwanted billing

**The pattern underneath:** almost every objection is a *timeline* objection dressed as a product objection. People are not rejecting therapy; they are rejecting a bargain whose payoff date nobody told them. The community's whole counter-argument is to reset the clock — "give it two weeks", "give it three months", "it took me a year" — plus a mask change.

## 7. Beliefs — the community's operating theory

A remarkably consistent folk model, repeated by hundreds of people in near-identical words. It functions as doctrine.

- **"It's all about the right mask."** Nearly every problem — leaks, sores, poor numbers, insomnia, quitting — is diagnosed as a mask problem first.
- **Under 5 is the goal.** AHI under 5 is the finish line; members post their nightly figure the way runners post splits. "Less than 5 events is the goal."
- **Non-use is fatal.** The reflex answer to doubt: "The cost could be your life." "Cheaper then your funeral." "Ten years off your life." Dementia and stroke are invoked constantly.
- **It's treatment, not cure.** The favoured analogy is eyeglasses: "they work instantly and while wearing them". A second framing: "you are taking medicine for your heart every time you use the machine."
- **Attitude is a therapy variable.** "The number one key to success with your CPAP machine is positive mental attitude from day one." Struggling is quietly reframed as a mindset failure.
- **Consumer data is untrustworthy; machine data is scripture.** "Apps are notoriously inaccurate" — yet advanced users dispute the machine's own scoring too, tracking awake-state artefacts in OSCAR and SleepHQ.
- **You must self-advocate or be forgotten.** "You have to be proactive in medicine these days." "GET A SECOND OPINION." Switching doctors and suppliers is normal, encouraged behaviour.
- **Numbers follow your life, not just your settings.** Stress, back pain, migraine medication, alcohol, a late meal and body position are all cited as causes of a bad night.

## 8. Emotion — the register

Five tones dominate, and they sit right next to each other in the same threads.

**Fear, stated literally**
> "I fell asleep on the couch last night (without the machine) and woke up gasping for breath. I really thought i was going to die."

**Grief and warning**
> "My husband went off a bit until he was told by pulmonary that being off, it will affect your heart… my husband died from his first and only heart attack."
> — widow, posting into a stranger's thread

**Despair — the quietest and most important tone**
> "probably once a month I cry before bed bc I do not want to pap up anymore… It's pretty much a life sentence without parole" — 19 months in

> "i'm fat and under the depression. Oftentimes I am hoping to just p@$$ away in my sleep."

> "I'm with you total torture device of the century" — two years on ASV, no relief

**Peer pressure, sometimes as shaming**
> "What is the cost? It's your life get use to it." · "Two night? Come on you can do better than that." · "Dont be so dramatic"
> — three separate replies to one person asking about returning a machine

**Triumph, dark humour and attachment**
> "I absolutely hated it. I absolutely hate that I need it. But I absolutely love to live. So I got used to it. And now I can't imagine sleeping without it. His name is Darth"

> "it's a game changer, if it had a finger I would put a ring on it"

## 10. Their words — swipe file

Use these rather than clinical translations. The group has its own dialect and reacts to it.

**How they name the thing**
- "pap up" — verb form: *"I do not want to pap up anymore"*
- "#happypapping" — the community's own sign-off
- "my CPAP journey" — near-universal framing of the first year
- "my numbers" / "my instances" / "events" — AHI, in three registers
- "the required 4 hours" — the nightly compliance minimum, treated as a target
- "rainout" — condensation in the hose and mask
- "sleep junk" — events they believe are artefacts, not apnea
- "fit pack" — the multi-size mask trial kit, often uncovered
- "DME" — the equipment supplier, named more often than the doctor

**Pain, in their words**
- "my body just will not relax"
- "gasping for air at 10k feet in Colorado"
- "my lips stick to my teeth"
- "ripping my mask off… and not remembering doing it"
- "throwing it across the room"
- "feeling like crap" / "feel absolutely miserable"
- "still waiting for the sleep fountain of youth"
- "a life sentence without parole"
- "I was afraid to drive"
- "total torture device of the century"

**Hope, in their words**
- "game changer" / "life saver" / "lifeline"
- "0.0 events" / "scored 2 x 100 in a row"
- "I can't sleep without it now"
- "I actually have dreams"
- "I no longer wake up gagging for breath"
- "it's lovely to get some good sleep again"
- "waking up feeling wonderful"

**Encouragement scripts**
- "hang in there" / "stick with it" / "don't give up"
- "give it time — it took me three months"
- "it's a process" / "trial and error"
- "it's all about the right mask"
- "the cost could be your life"
- "celebrate every small win. Baby steps."
- "practice wearing it while you're watching tv"

## 11. Open gaps — where unmet need concentrates

Each of these is currently being handled by strangers on Facebook.

1. **A real first-90-nights programme.** Expectation-setting, a night-by-night plan, desensitisation practice, and a human to escalate to. The group already runs this informally; nobody has productised it.
2. **Mask selection that isn't guesswork.** Face scanning exists at some suppliers and members report it beats in-person fitting — but fit packs are frequently not covered and exchanges are capped at one. Skin damage is the cost of the current process.
3. **Plain-language interpretation of their own data.** Not another dashboard. Answers to the questions actually being asked: what a pressure of 16 means, why a leak reads 79 L/min when nothing is audible, whether 2.8 events is good on day three. People are asking ChatGPT and strangers, and being told off for it.
4. **A pathway for the compliant-but-still-tired.** The single largest source of despair and the clearest trigger for abandonment. Peers reflexively suggest ferritin, thyroid, oximetry and hormones — that triage is real and unowned.
5. **Mouth-leak resolution as a designed solution.** Tape, chin straps, hybrid masks, XyliMelts, humidity tuning — dozens of partial fixes and no accepted answer, for a symptom that causes tooth decay.
6. **Psychological support for mask anxiety.** Panic, claustrophobia, PTSD associations and sensory sensitivity all described. CBT-I is mentioned by a few informed members and is clearly under-referred.
7. **Trustworthy direct purchase.** Repeated, explicit, cash-in-hand demand: a supplier that doesn't auto-bill, gets orders right, and lets people control their own settings.
8. **Clear, jurisdiction-specific answers on driving.** The licence thread produced contradictory advice from every country and several US states — and one member advising others to hide symptoms from their doctor. That is a safety problem created by an information gap.

## Method & limits

**What was read.** The public feed of the Global Sleep Apnea Support Community (96,236 members), sorted chronologically and scrolled to roughly seven days of history — about 150 distinct posts with their top-level comments — plus ten complete comment threads opened individually, including the 269-reply thread on returning a machine and the 57-reply thread on CPAP and weight gain. Read-only throughout: nothing was posted, liked, voted, or messaged.

**Quoting.** Quotes are verbatim fragments, spelling and typos preserved, kept short deliberately. Members are identified by role and situation rather than by name, since these are identifiable individuals discussing their health.

**Skew to keep in mind.** This is a self-selected support group, so it over-represents people struggling in the first year and people who have made peace with therapy, and under-represents the people who quietly quit — the most commercially interesting cohort is the one that isn't posting. Facebook throttled comment loading partway through collection, so a few large threads are captured only to their first ~40 comments. Geography skews US, UK, Canada and Australia; language is English; the age signal in a group poll clustered in the 40s and 60-plus. Frequency labels describe how often themes appeared in this sample, not verified population prevalence.

**Not medical guidance.** Everything here is a record of what community members said to each other, including their folk theories and their disagreements. Several widely-repeated beliefs in this document are contested or wrong.
