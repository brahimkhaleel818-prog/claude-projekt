## The five findings that matter most

1. **This is two markets, not one.** People deciding what's wrong with them (diagnosis 29%, fatigue 24% of r/SleepApnea) and people operating a device they already own (leaks 14.5%, cleaning 12.7% of r/CPAP) share almost no vocabulary.
2. **The largest under-served pain is "successfully treated and still destroyed."** AHI below 1, perfect adherence, still exhausted — and dismissed because the numbers look good. It appears independently on Reddit and CPAPtalk, and one person calls their own machine "gaslighting me".
3. **The deepest emotional wound is self-blame, not tiredness.** Years of believing they were lazy. Rare in raw counts, overwhelming in intensity.
4. **Fear-based messaging is a trap.** This audience already knows about strokes; being told again is the thing they resent most.
5. **The partner is a buyer, not just an influencer** — and partner-bought solutions that fail make the patient more hostile, not less.
6. **The clearest commercial vacuum is support after the handoff.** One post asking "what support did you wish you had in the first month?" drew 211 replies. People are handed a complex medical device with a QR code and left alone.

## Honest limitations

- **No Facebook data.** No groups were named or made accessible, and group content is login-walled. Methodology §5 explains how to add that layer.
- Reddit blocks direct automated access from this environment; content came from a public Reddit archive with every original permalink preserved.
- Comments were collected thread-by-thread from the 163 most-discussed posts (11,528 of them), so they represent the market's busiest conversations rather than a random slice. No post-level frequency claim uses comment data.
- Everything here reflects English-language, online, help-seeking patients. People who quietly quit treatment stop posting — this corpus systematically under-counts them.

## Account safety

Nothing was posted, commented, liked, reacted to, messaged, joined, left, edited or configured on any platform. No login was used. All access was anonymous, read-only retrieval of public content.
