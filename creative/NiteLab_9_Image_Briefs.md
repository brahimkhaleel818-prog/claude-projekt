## PRE-FLIGHT — FOUR CHANGES SINCE THE CONCEPT DOC

**1. The guarantee is confirmed: 90 days, money back.** The packaging shot resolves the placeholder. Every CTA below now reads **90-day money-back guarantee** instead of "30-night risk-free." This strengthens the objection-killer in all three top concepts, because O1 (irreversibility) is the market's operative objection and 90 days is a materially stronger answer than 30.

**2. Concept 2B — "Start there. Not with a machine." → flagged and changed.** In a snoring ad seen by CPAP users, "not with a machine" can read as *don't use your prescribed therapy*. **Compliant replacement: "Start with the noise."** Same idea, no implied comparison to prescribed treatment.

**3. Concept 3C — the left panel → flagged and changed.** The original showed strap headgear labelled "What he refused," which reads as CPAP even unlabelled. **Compliant replacement:** the left panel shows a **generic sporting-goods boil-and-bite mouthguard tray in a drawer**, labelled *"What he gave up on."* Keeps the acceptance-cost mechanism, removes the prescribed-therapy comparison entirely.

**4. Product facts locked for accuracy.** Clear/transparent upper and lower trays with blue trim, blue lateral adjustment rails, hinged, supplied in a white oval case, blue foil pouch, blue-and-white cleaning brush. **The attached product photo is the source of truth in every prompt.**

**Standing rules applied to all nine:** 1080×1080, all key elements inside a 90px safe margin · one dominant message · no OSA/AHI/oxygen/cardiovascular/CPAP-replacement claims · no "cure," "eliminate," "silent" absolutes · Meta personal-attributes safe (no copy implying the viewer has a diagnosed condition) · no exaggerated distress expressions.

# CONCEPT 1 — "IT WASN'T YOU"

**ANGLE:** Nobody Ever Fitted You
**SUB-AVATAR:** SA-1, The Unfitted (snoring-device buyer)
**AWARENESS LEVEL:** Solution-Aware — entered through a mechanism reveal
**VARIATION TYPE:** A — Pain / Pattern Recognition

**FINAL HEADLINE:** It wasn't you. It was one-size-fits-all.
**FINAL SUPPORTING LINE:** Three mouthguards in a drawer says the mouthguards were wrong.
**FINAL BENEFIT 1:** 25 settings you adjust yourself
**FINAL BENEFIT 2:** No boiling, no molding, no dentist
**FINAL BENEFIT 3:** 90-day money-back guarantee
**FINAL CTA:** Adjust it to your mouth — $59.95

**VISUAL STORY:** One open bathroom drawer, photographed from directly above, holding the evidence of every failed attempt — and one thing that isn't failed yet. The moment is recognition: this customer owns this drawer, and the ad accuses the drawer instead of them.

**SUBJECT:** No people. The drawer contents are the subject: two used yellowed boil-and-bite mouthguard trays with visible bite impressions, a crumpled box of nasal strips with two loose strips beside it, a half-used roll of white mouth tape, a black hair tie, a nail clipper, one loose button. Objects sit at natural angles — nothing arranged.

**ENVIRONMENT:** A middle-class American bathroom vanity drawer, laminate-lined, slightly scuffed. Faint water staining in one corner. Drawer edges show wear. A folded washcloth at the back, partially out of frame.

**LIGHTING:** Ordinary overhead bathroom lighting from directly above and slightly forward — flat, honest, slightly cool white. Soft real shadows under each object. No studio softbox, no rim light, no glow.

**CAMERA / COMPOSITION:** Straight overhead flat-lay, 35mm, camera parallel to the drawer, shot handheld so the framing is very slightly off-square. Drawer fills the lower two-thirds of the frame. Natural depth — everything essentially in focus, no bokeh.

**NITELAB PRODUCT PLACEMENT:** Lower-right of the drawer, sitting in its open white oval case, clear trays with blue trim clearly visible, clean and undamaged — the only object in the drawer that looks current. Roughly 15% of the frame. Deliberately not hero-sized; it reads as "the next one," not "the winner."

**TEXT LAYOUT:** Headline in the top third on a solid deep-navy band that spans the full width, two lines, left-aligned, generous padding. Supporting line directly beneath the band in smaller white type on the photo. Three benefits as short check-marked lines stacked bottom-left over the darker drawer area. CTA as a rounded blue pill, bottom-centre. Small white NiteLab wordmark bottom-right.

**DESIGN DIRECTION:** Clean, premium DTC. Rounded geometric sans (Quicksand-like) for the headline at high weight; neutral sans for benefits. Strong size contrast: headline dominant, supporting line ~45% of headline size, benefits ~30%. Generous line spacing. No drop shadows, no gradients, no starbursts, no price explosions.

**REALISM REQUIREMENTS:** Real plastic wear on the old trays — slight discoloration, scuffs, one with a visible thumb-pressed dent. Cardboard box slightly crushed at one corner. Tape roll not perfectly round. Dust in the drawer's corner seam. Micro-scratches on the laminate. Honest flat lighting with mild sensor noise in the shadowed corners.

**REFERENCE AD:** **R3** (the "finally free" layout) — borrow the skeleton only: headline in the top block, three short stacked benefits low-left, CTA pill at the base, small brand mark. Replace R3's face-with-device photo with the object flat-lay, which delivers pattern recognition faster than a portrait can.

**AVOID:** Styled or "curated" flat-lay arrangement · symmetrical object spacing · glossy new-looking old mouthguards · dramatic side lighting · shallow depth of field · dark moody grading · any competitor logo or recognizable brand on the failed products · showing a CPAP mask.

**CLAIM CHECK:** Passes. No medical claim of any kind. "One-size-fits-all" is a design statement about generic devices, not a health claim. 90-day guarantee is verified on packaging. $59.95 matches the current offer.

**LIGHTING:** Soft directional daylight from a window camera-left, with a gentle natural falloff to the right. Real specular highlights on the clear plastic. Slight unevenness. No ring light, no dual studio strobes.

**CAMERA / COMPOSITION:** Eye-level macro-ish product shot, 50mm equivalent, moderate close focus with only mild background blur. Hands and product occupy the centre-right; the left third is clean space for type. Very slight handheld tilt.

**NITELAB PRODUCT PLACEMENT:** The hero, but small-scale hero — sharply in focus, held naturally, showing the true clear trays, blue trim and blue lateral adjustment rail. Roughly 30% of the frame. Must match the reference photo exactly.

**TEXT LAYOUT:** Headline upper-left on clean negative space over the background, two lines. Supporting line directly beneath, lighter weight. Three benefits as a short check-marked column down the lower-left. CTA pill bottom-centre. Small NiteLab wordmark bottom-right.

**CLAIM CHECK:** Passes. "Setting 9" is presented as one person's setting, not a recommendation. "Talk and drink with it in" and "thinner than a credit card" are brand-described features, framed as features — no efficacy or medical claim. "Most people find theirs in the first week" is a soft usage statement; if you want zero exposure, change to "25 settings. Yours is in there."

# CONCEPT 3 — "$2,000 VS. 30 SECONDS"

**ANGLE:** Nobody Ever Fitted You
**SUB-AVATAR:** SA-1, specifically the person who priced a dentist-made device and walked away
**AWARENESS LEVEL:** Solution-Aware moving to Product-Aware
**VARIATION TYPE:** C — Mechanism / Contrarian Insight

**FINAL HEADLINE:** The dentist's version costs $2,000. The adjustment takes 30 seconds.
**FINAL SUPPORTING LINE:** Same principle. You do the adjusting.
**FINAL BENEFIT 1:** No impressions, no lab, no appointments
**FINAL BENEFIT 2:** Re-adjust any night, free
**FINAL BENEFIT 3:** $59.95 · 90-day money back
**FINAL CTA:** Skip the chair — start tonight

**VISUAL STORY:** Two photographs side by side that compare a *process*, not two brands: the clinical route on the left, the bedside route on the right. The insight lands before a word is read — one takes weeks and an appointment, one takes a nightstand.

**SUBJECT:** No faces. Left: a dental operatory detail — the arm of a dental chair, a stainless impression tray on a blue bib, an overhead operatory light out of focus behind. Right: an ordinary bedside nightstand at night with the NiteLab case open, a glass of water, reading glasses folded, a phone charging cable.

**ENVIRONMENT:** Left is clinical and cool: pale cabinetry, sterile surfaces, hard edges, faintly institutional. Right is domestic and warm: wood-grain nightstand with a ring stain, a paperback with a bent cover, a slightly dusty lamp base.

**LIGHTING:** Left — flat cool fluorescent/LED clinical lighting, slightly green-blue cast, hard small shadows. Right — warm low bedside lamp light from just outside the frame, pooled and uneven, deep soft shadows at the edges. The lighting difference does most of the persuasion.

**CAMERA / COMPOSITION:** Hard vertical split down the centre, 50/50. Both halves shot at eye level, 35mm, from a normal standing/sitting distance. No wide-angle distortion. Each half has its own small caption block.

**NITELAB PRODUCT PLACEMENT:** Right half only, on the nightstand in its open white case with the clear blue-trimmed trays visible, at a natural size — about 12% of the total frame. Not enlarged, not floating.

**TEXT LAYOUT:** Headline spans the full width across the top over a semi-transparent dark band, two lines, centred. Supporting line beneath in smaller type. Under each photo half, a small caption: left "$2,000 · weeks · appointments", right "$59.95 · tonight · 25 settings". Three benefits in a single row of check-marked short phrases just above the CTA. CTA pill bottom-centre. Wordmark bottom-right.

**DESIGN DIRECTION:** Diagnostic and matter-of-fact. Thin 2px white divider down the centre. Captions in small caps, letter-spaced. Headline authoritative but not aggressive. Cool-vs-warm color contrast between halves is the main design device — do not neutralize it in post.

**REALISM REQUIREMENTS:** Real clinical clutter on the left — a smudge on the tray, a slightly crooked bib, a visible power cord. Real domestic wear on the right — dust on the lamp base, a water ring on the wood, a crease in the paperback cover, a slightly tangled charging cable. Both halves must look photographed on the same camera by the same person.

**REFERENCE AD:** **R1** (the split-screen). Borrow the hard vertical split, the paired short caption lists and the CTA at the base — but the comparison is **process vs process**, never brand vs brand, which keeps it defensible and avoids naming or showing a competitor device.

**AVOID:** Any recognizable dental brand, logo or practice name · a dentist's face or a patient in the chair · making the clinical side look sinister or dirty (it should look competent, just slow and expensive) · CGI-looking dental equipment · symmetrical mirrored composition · price starbursts or "SALE" graphics.

**CLAIM CHECK:** Passes with one guardrail. The $1,500–$3,000 custom-device range is verified in the fact-check, so "$2,000" is fair. "Same principle" is the honest ceiling — **do not** upgrade it to "same results," "as effective as a dentist device," or "clinically equivalent." No implication that dentist-made devices are a scam. No apnea language anywhere.
