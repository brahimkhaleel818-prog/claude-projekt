# ⚠️ READ FIRST — THE PRODUCT/ANGLE FIT PROBLEM

All three selected angles come from a **diagnosed sleep-apnea, CPAP-using** population. NiteLab is an **anti-snoring mouthpiece for primary (simple) snoring** and, per your own fact-check, **"is not a treatment for obstructive sleep apnea."** That gap is not a detail — it decides what each angle can and cannot say.

| Angle | Original avatar | Fit with NiteLab | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Nobody Ever Fitted You** | SA-1, CPAP user whose *mask* was never fitted | The *object* doesn't transfer (NiteLab doesn't fit CPAP masks). The *psychology* transfers exactly: self-blame from a bad fit → relief; and the objection (irreversible purchase) is answered by 25 settings + risk-free trial. | **Run — but translated.** Target the snorer whose *device* never fit (boil-and-bite, one-size, $2,000 dentist quote), not the CPAP patient. |

Selling a snoring mouthpiece as the answer to their residual exhaustion is an unsupported medical claim, a Meta policy risk, and potentially harmful. | **Do not run as written.** I have built the three variations at the only defensible version — the *undiagnosed / "told it's mild"* snorer — and scored the fit honestly. |
| **3 — You're the One Who's Awake** | SA-5, the bed partner | **Strongest fit in the set.** Snoring noise is exactly what the partner experiences and exactly what the product addresses. The partner is a documented buyer. | **Run.** Best product/message fit of the three. |

**Three hard guardrails applied to every concept below:**

1. **No OSA claims.** No "treats sleep apnea," no AHI, no oxygen, no cardiovascular outcomes, no "replace your CPAP." Where a CPAP user is implied, the ad speaks to people who **snore and don't want a machine** — never to diagnosed patients abandoning prescribed therapy.
2. **No borrowed apnea drama.** The most emotionally powerful partner material in the research — counting the gaps, "oxygen plummeting below 70%," watching him stop breathing — is **apnea evidence and is off-limits for this product**. Angle 3 uses snoring noise and the relationship cost only.
3. **Meta personal-attributes policy.** Copy must not assert or imply knowledge of the viewer's medical condition. "Still exhausted despite your CPAP?" implies a diagnosis. Every headline below is written as a general statement, a partner's observation, or an "if/anyone" construction.

# STEP 1 — THE THREE CUSTOMER CHAINS (reconstructed, unmixed)

## CHAIN 1 — SA-1, THE UNFITTED (translated to the snoring-device buyer)

- **Core Avatar** — The Reluctantly Self-Reliant Patient: gets handed a device and left to work it out alone.
- **Sub-Avatar** — SA-1, The Unfitted.
- **Defining Experience** — Something was chosen for them that didn't fit their face or mouth, and nobody watched them use it. *"this is the mask we start everyone with."* · *"look, and touch but don't dare try them on."* Translated to this category: a one-size boil-and-bite that hurt or fell out, or a $1,500–$3,000 dentist quote they walked away from.
- **Surface Pain** — It leaks / falls out / hurts. It doesn't work and they don't know why.
- **Deep Pain** — *"I've thought surely I'm doing something wrong."*
- **Surface Desire** — *"why don't they give you a bunch of different masks to try out a few nights each?"* → in this category: something they can actually adjust to their own mouth.
- **Deep Desire** — For the failure to be **mechanical, not personal.**
- **Failed Solutions** — 3 to 13 masks in the original; here: nasal strips, mouth tape, special pillows, cheap boil-and-bite trays, and the dentist device they couldn't justify. *"$ down the drain."*
- **Existing Belief** — *"it's all about the right mask"* → **"it's all about the right fit."**
- **Main Objection** — **O1, irreversibility.** *"I get one exchange and I am afraid the next mask might be worse."* · *"no take-backs."*
- **Problem Mechanism (PM1)** — The interface was chosen for the average person, not for their anatomy.
- **Solution Mechanism (SM1 + SM5)** — Adjustability they control, plus a way to send it back. In the original: several masks at home with returns. In this category: **25 settings + risk-free trial.**
- **Awareness** — Solution-Aware, entered by mechanism reveal.

## CHAIN 2 — SA-2, THE NON-RESPONDER (defensible version only)

- **Core Avatar** — The Reluctantly Self-Reliant Patient.
- **Sub-Avatar** — SA-2 in its **non-diagnosed** form: the person told *"it's too mild to be causing any symptoms for me"*, or never worked up at all. **Not the CPAP-compliant patient.**
- **Defining Experience** — Their complaint was measured, filed and dismissed. *"'well your AHI is down.'"* → here: *"your study came back mild."*
- **Surface Pain** — Still snoring, still waking up feeling like they didn't sleep.
- **Deep Pain** — *"Im doing GREAT....but I dont feel great."* The last explanation has been spent.
- **Surface Desire** — *"Is there any test or anything which can determine that why am i still feeling tired?"*
- **Deep Desire** — To be believed. Then **certainty** — the winning answer in the 96k-member poll.
- **Failed Solutions** — Sleep hygiene, sleep aids, *"just get used to it"*, and every drugstore snoring gadget.
- **Existing Belief** — *"You can absolutely feel like death with just a 'mild' diagnosis!"*
- **Main Objection** — **O2, promise fatigue.** *"In my experience the better more refreshing sleep is a lie."*
- **Problem Mechanism** — **PM2 is the research's mechanism and NiteLab cannot answer it.** The only mechanism NiteLab legitimately owns here is the proven one: snoring is soft tissue vibrating in a narrowed airway.
- **Solution Mechanism** — SM4 (measure and explain what they feel) is **not something a mouthpiece delivers.** This is the fit break.
- **Awareness** — Problem-Aware, Solution-Unaware.

## CHAIN 3 — SA-5, THE BED PARTNER

- **Core Avatar** — Adjacent: the person sleeping next to the patient.
- **Sub-Avatar** — SA-5, The Bed Partner.
- **Defining Experience** — Lying awake next to the noise, night after night, and eventually leaving the room.
- **Surface Pain** — Snoring, no sleep, separate bedrooms. *"we have been together for three years, and have never spent a night in the same bed."*
- **Deep Pain** — Being told to accommodate it. *"He's told me that I need to figure out a way to sleep comfortably next to him. He suggested I could wear headphones to bed."*
- **Surface Desire** — Something he will actually use.
- **Deep Desire** — To share a bed again, and to stop being the only one awake.
- **Failed Solutions** — Nagging, earplugs, headphones, the spare room — and **his abandoned attempts**: *"He tried using the C-Pap a few times but gave up because it was too uncomfortable for him."*
- **Existing Belief** — He won't wear anything strapped to his face.
- **Main Objection** — He'll refuse and the money is wasted. *"he still says it's a scam and a waste of money."*
- **Problem Mechanism** — He can't hear himself; she's the only witness, with no authority.
- **Solution Mechanism** — Something low-commitment enough that he'll actually try it — answered by a device with no mask, no hose, no machine, plus a risk-free trial that removes her downside.
- **Awareness** — Problem-Aware on his behalf, Solution-Aware on her own.

## VARIATION 1A — "It Wasn't You"
**Approach:** Pain / pattern recognition

- **ANGLE** — Nobody Ever Fitted You
- **SUB-AVATAR** — SA-1, The Unfitted (snoring-device buyer)
- **AWARENESS LEVEL** — Solution-Aware. They've bought in this category before. The claim asks them to re-attribute the failure from themselves to the device.
- **CREATIVE STRATEGY** — Test whether removing self-blame outperforms any product claim. The ad's whole job is the sentence the research says they've never heard: it wasn't you.
- **HOOK / HEADLINE** — **"It wasn't you. It was one-size-fits-all."**
- **SUPPORTING LINE** — "Three mouthguards in a drawer says the mouthguards were wrong."
- **3 BENEFITS** — ✓ 25 settings you adjust yourself · ✓ No boiling, no molding, no dentist · ✓ Not right? Send it back
- **PRODUCT BRIDGE** — If the last one failed because it was built for an average mouth, the answer is one that adjusts to yours.
- **VISUAL CONCEPT** — Top third: bold headline on a deep navy block. Below: an honest overhead shot of a bathroom drawer — two used boil-and-bite trays, a box of nasal strips, a roll of mouth tape — shot flat, slightly messy, real. NiteLab sits at the drawer's edge, clean and small, not hero-sized. Benefits as three check lines bottom-left. CTA pill bottom-centre. **No person in frame** (avoids the self-perception issue entirely). Feeling: recognition, not shame — a drawer everyone in this segment owns.
- **REFERENCE AD** — **R3.** Same skeleton: headline top-left, stacked short benefits, CTA at the base. Swapping the sleeping face for an object shot is the only change, and it makes the pattern-recognition idea land faster.
- **WHY THIS SHOULD RESONATE** — **Pain:** devices that don't fit and are blamed on the user. **Desire:** for the failure to be mechanical, not personal. **Belief:** "it's all about the right fit." **Objection:** O1 — "no take-backs" — answered by the third benefit. **Mechanism:** PM1 translated — the device was built for the average mouth. Your own product page already says this out loud: *"it's not your fault."*
- **CLAIM / COMPLIANCE CHECK** — Clean. No health claim of any kind. Confirm the return window and state the exact number of days ("30-Day Risk-Free Trial") rather than a vague "send it back." No competitor product shown or named.
