# CUSTOMER MODEL — Consolidated pains, desires, beliefs, objections, triggers, fears

Interpretation, built on the evidence in `CUSTOMER-RESEARCH/`. Each item carries a confidence label. Where the market splits, both sides are kept.

---

## 3. FUNCTIONAL DESIRES

- Sleep through one entire night with the therapy on
- Wake up rested — "the 'finally rested' feeling that others have described"
- Stop the leak without over-tightening into pain
- Stop the bloating, the dry mouth, the sores
- Get a diagnosis without a lab and a stranger watching them sleep
- Know what is normal in the first weeks and what is fixable
- An explanation when the numbers are good and they still feel awful
- A treatment that survives travel, side-sleeping, beards, and real life
- Buy supplies without a prescription fight

Confidence: **High** — all are stated directly and repeatedly.

## 4. DEEPER EMOTIONAL DESIRES

- **Vindication:** to learn they were never lazy or weak
- **To be believed:** over the chart, over the AHI, over the stereotype
- **Normality:** to be someone who just sleeps, not someone who operates a machine
- **Safety:** to stop being afraid of being unconscious
- **Reconnection:** to share a bed with their partner again without dread
- **Competence restored:** to be reliable, present and sharp again — at work, with their kids
- **Not to become a patient:** to keep the identity they had before diagnosis

Confidence: **Medium-High.** These are inferred from consistent language across independent people, which is exactly what interpretation is — treat them as well-supported readings, not measured facts.

## 5. FAILED SOLUTIONS (and why they failed, in their words)

| Tried | Why it failed for them | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| More sleep, naps, caffeine, "sleep hygiene" | Treats the symptom, not the airway | **High** |
| Antidepressants, thyroid panels, iron, fibro/CFS labels | Wrong diagnosis; years lost | **Medium-High** |
| CPAP — 3 to 11 masks | Leak vs. sores trade-off; can't fall asleep; rips it off unconsciously | **High** |
| Chin straps | "Didn't help, just angered him more"; TMJ pain reported | **Medium-High** |
| Mouth tape | Works until it doesn't; wet tape, dry mouth; impossible for blocked noses | **Medium** |
| Oral appliance / MAD | Tooth and jaw pain; partial effect; cost | **Medium-High** |
| Positional therapy, wedge pillows, tennis-ball tricks | Insufficient at higher severity; can't retrain 50 years of side-sleeping | **Medium** |
| Inspire implant | Surgical pain, persistent lump, benefit faded, "zapping", billing shock | **Medium** (few but detailed accounts) |
| ENT surgery (UPPP, septoplasty, MMA, tonsillectomy) | **Genuinely bimodal** — "the worst and best thing I ever did" | **Medium** |
| Weight loss / GLP-1 | Worked completely for some; irrelevant and insulting to normal-BMI patients | **High** that both outcomes are real |
| Stimulants prescribed for residual sleepiness | "If I was sleeping well I wouldn't need stimulants" | **Medium** |

## 6. OBJECTIONS (what stops them buying)

1. **"I've already tried everything like that."** The most common. Anything resembling an accessory they've already bought is dead on arrival.
2. **"It'll sit in a drawer like the last one."** Sunk-cost fatigue, often literal — $1,200 machines in closets.
3. **"You're sponsored."** Positive content is assumed paid until proven otherwise.
4. **"My insurance won't cover it / I can't afford another thing."**
5. **"My numbers are fine, so nobody will help me anyway."** (SA-4's learned helplessness)
6. **"I'm not the type / this isn't aimed at someone like me."** (thin, young, female, fit)
7. **"Don't lecture me about strokes."** Risk-based pressure reads as condescension.
8. **"Is this even legitimate?"** Prescription/regulatory uncertainty around anything sold direct.

Confidence: **High** for 1–4 and 7; **Medium-High** for 5, 6, 8.

## 7. BELIEFS (held sincerely, whether true or not)

- CPAP is the only thing doctors will offer.
- A good AHI means the therapy is working *for me* — so if I feel bad, something else is wrong with me.
- Mild apnea isn't serious. (Repeatedly precedes abandonment of treatment.)
- Everyone is tired; this is normal adult life.
- Losing weight would fix it. (True for some, false and insulting for others.)
- Surgery is a permanent, clean fix. (Contradicted by the accounts collected here.)
- The DME/insurance system is adversarial and wasteful.
- Watch-based apnea detection is unreliable.

## 8. MISCONCEPTIONS worth knowing

- That pressurised air "inflates" the body (sincerely asked, heavily upvoted)
- That the four-hour compliance threshold is a clinical target rather than an insurance rule
- That failing CPAP means failing treatment altogether
- That not snoring rules out apnea
- That an "official" mild classification reflects how bad the nights actually are (the REM-concentrated case in `01` A3 is the clearest counter-example)

## 9. AWARENESS LEVELS

| Level | Who | Marker language |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Chronically tired, no suspicion of apnea | "I just assume everyone is always exhausted" |
| Problem-aware | Suspects something is wrong; partner or watch has flagged it | "Do I have sleep apnea?" |
| Solution-aware | Diagnosed, weighing options | "How much of a difference does a CPAP make?" |
| Product-aware | Owns the device, comparing masks and alternatives | "N20 vs F40", "should I try a MAD" |
| Most aware | Optimisers and the treated-but-not-better | "AHI <1, flow limitation, what am I missing?" |

## 10. COMMON TRIGGERS (what makes them act)

Measured on the unbiased sample; see `CUSTOMER-RESEARCH/03` Part 2.

1. Another health finding — blood pressure, heart rhythm, oxygen (**7.6%**) — **High**
2. A smartwatch/phone apnea notification (**3.8%**) — **High**, and rising
3. A doctor initiating it (**1.3%**) — **Medium**
4. A partner's alarm at witnessed apneas or snoring (**0.7% explicit**, far more in narrative) — **Medium-High**
5. A frightening near-miss — waking gasping or choking (**0.2%**) — **Low-Medium**
6. A life event: a new relationship, a pregnancy, a job with a driving licence at stake — **Low**

## 11. COMMON FEARS

- Dying in their sleep, or not waking up (rare in words, heavy in weight)
- Stroke, heart attack, atrial fibrillation, long-term brain damage
- Being on a machine forever
- Their partner leaving, or the marriage eroding
- Losing a job or a driving licence to daytime sleepiness
- That the treatment will fail them and nothing else will be offered
- That they will never feel rested again — that this is now their baseline

## 12. COMMON FRUSTRATIONS

- Waiting: months for a study, months for a follow-up, years for a diagnosis
- Being handed a device with no instruction ("a scan code linked to a youtube video")
- Insurance rules that gate treatment on hours worn rather than benefit felt
- DME suppliers, prescriptions that expire, supplies that stop
- Doctors who read the chart instead of the person
- The trade-off trap: every fix creates a new problem
- Family and strangers who treat the condition as a joke or a character flaw
- Products that assume you sleep on your back, clean-shaven, alone, in one position

## 13. Conflicts preserved (deliberately not averaged)

| Group A says | Group B says | Both real because |
|---|---|---|
| "CPAP saved my life, best money I ever spent" | "CPAP made everything worse, I dread bedtime" | Different anatomy, different adaptation, different support — 3.2% post success stories, 11.6–14.2% post struggle |
| "I lost weight and my apnea is gone" | "I have a BMI of 19 and an AHI of 19" | Both documented; weight is causal for some and irrelevant for others |
| "Fear of stroke is what keeps me compliant" | "Don't lecture me about strokes, I already know" | Fear motivates the newly diagnosed and insults the long-struggling |
| "Surgery gave me my life back" | "Inspire failed and I still have a lump" | Surgical outcomes genuinely are bimodal |
| "The data proves therapy is working" | "The data is gaslighting me" | AHI measures events, not how a person feels |

Anyone building a single message for "sleep apnea sufferers" will hit at least one of these fault lines. Segment first, then write.
